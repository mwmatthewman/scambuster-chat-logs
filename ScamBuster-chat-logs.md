## Please summarize the purpose of scambuster

ScamBuster stalls telemarketers. It keeps them talking and never agrees to buy, so the pitch dies on the line instead of on you.

The phone itself stays free. A website cannot answer or hold an Android call, so the pitch has to be forwarded off the line. The home screen says “Keep them talking. Never say yes.”

It does that with moods on the line, a practice pitch recorded as a shareable WAV, up to four stall lines at once, and carrier divert for T-Mobile, AT&T, and Verizon.

## How is the call forwarded off the line and what does the user need to allow that to hapoen?

The app does not take the call. It opens the Phone app and dials a carrier forwarding code. The carrier then sends the call to another number, so your handset never stays on the line.

On the Divert screen you pick T-Mobile, AT&T, or Verizon and enter a 10-digit US number that should answer. Three links become active:

| Button | What it dials | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Send every call | T-Mobile/AT&T `*21*<number>#`, Verizon `*72<number>` | Your phone stops ringing. Every call goes to that number. |
| Only if you don't pick up | T-Mobile/AT&T `*61*<number>#`, Verizon `*92<number>` | Your phone rings first. If you let it go, the call leaves your line. |
| Stop forwarding | T-Mobile/AT&T `#21#`, Verizon `*73` | Cancels send-all forwarding. |

**Send every call** stops your phone from ringing. **Only if you don't pick up** rings you first, then sends the call away if you do not answer.

Tapping one of those opens the Phone app with that code already filled in. You place the call and wait for the carrier to confirm. A website cannot set this in the background, and the app does not need the microphone for this step.

What you need:

- A real T-Mobile, AT&T, or Verizon line. An emulator will not complete the code.
- A second number you control, one that will actually pick up and talk. ScamBuster on this phone cannot be that line.
- Permission for the page to open the Phone app, then you confirm the dial.

Do not use **Send every call** unless that second number is yours. It stops your phone from ringing. After a test, tap **Stop forwarding** and wait for the carrier to confirm.

## Dont check the notes, check the app.

The app does not take the call. On the home screen, **Send the call away** opens Divert. That page builds a carrier code and puts it in a `tel:` link. Tapping the link opens the Phone app with the code filled in. You place that call. The carrier, not the website, turns forwarding on.

Nothing in the app can set this by itself. There is no microphone prompt and no forwarding permission on that screen. What you have to allow is the Phone app opening and the dial going through, then wait for the carrier to confirm.

The number you enter has to be a real line that will pick up and talk. The app says it cannot be that line. Android gives the call to the Phone app, and this site cannot answer it, join it, or hold the telemarketer on your handset.

## I want the app to be able to function without having to forward the call, without using the device speaker, and to be able to answer the call if the user presses a prompt while the call is still ringing. Preprogramming the app based on categories is not the intended function.

The app cannot do that. Android will not let this site answer a ringing call or put a voice on the line without the speaker or a forward.

What the app does now is separate from the phone call. **Send the call away** only opens the Phone app and dials a carrier code. **Practice a pitch** and the live lines stay inside the page: they listen with the microphone and play replies out of the speaker. Leaving the page drops both. Moods such as Excited or Skeptical, and the Warranty, Solar, Benefits, and Debt prompts, are stored scripts. None of that picks up the handset.

Answering while it is still ringing is allowed only for the phone’s default dialer app, and only after you set that app as the default. This site cannot be that dialer. Even a real dialer app still cannot feed audio into a carrier call or hear the caller’s audio. Android sends call audio through the modem, not through an app. The only outputs an app can use are the speaker or a headset, which is the path you do not want.

So there is no change inside this app that answers the call, stays off the speaker, and skips forwarding. That would need a native phone app, and Android still blocks the audio.

## The idea is that when a user gets a call from an unknown number, they can press a button, and the app will take over, whike leaving the user free to continue to use the device for calls or internet browsing

That only works if this phone never stays on the call. One line cannot talk to the telemarketer and place another call at the same time. Browsing can happen during a call. Another call cannot.

The current app does not do the one-tap handoff you described. **Send the call away** turns on a standing carrier forward before any call arrives, and it sends every call, not just this unknown one. The pitch lines inside the app never touch the ringing call. They use the microphone and the speaker, and they stop when you leave the page.

A button while an unknown number is still ringing has to live in the Phone app. This site cannot see that call, read the number, or answer it. Only an app set as the phone’s default dialer can.

Even that dialer cannot keep the conversation on this phone and free the line. Android will not let an app speak into a carrier call or hear the caller except through the speaker or a headset. To leave you free for other calls, this one call has to be handed to a different number that does the talking. Android has a one-call handoff for the default dialer, and most US carriers do not offer it. The handoff they do offer is the standing forward already in the app.

## So how do you make this work given the constraints?

The phone never takes the call. A second number, owned by the app, answers and talks. This phone only decides whether to send the call there.

That is the only setup that leaves you free to make other calls and browse, and it does not use the speaker. One line cannot talk to the telemarketer and place another call at the same time. Android will not let an app speak into a carrier call or hear the caller, so the talking has to happen on a normal phone line the app controls.

On a call it works like this:

1. An unknown number rings your phone as usual. You can still answer it yourself.
2. If you do not pick up, the carrier sends that call to ScamBuster’s number. Your line stays idle.
3. That number answers, hears what the caller actually says, and talks back. It does not pick a mood or a script such as Warranty or Solar. It never agrees to buy and never gives a card or identity number.

The setup is conditional forwarding, once: “only if I don’t answer,” aimed at ScamBuster’s number. Not “send every call.” On Verizon that is `*71` plus the number. On T-Mobile it is `**61*1` plus the number and `#`. On AT&T it is the phone’s “forward when unanswered” setting. Your voicemail will not get the calls that take this path. Turn it off the same way the carrier turns conditional forwarding off.

What this app does not have is that answering number. Divert only dials the forward code to a number you type, and the app cannot be the line that picks up. The practice screen is a rehearsal in the browser. It is not the live call.

A button on the ringing screen that says “take this one” is not available. US carriers do not offer a one-call handoff while the phone is still ringing, and this site cannot see the incoming call. Declining a call is not the same as “busy,” so it will not reliably send the call anywhere. Not answering is the action that works.
